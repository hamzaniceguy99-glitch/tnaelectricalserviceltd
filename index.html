We work to BS 7671 and are registered with a government-authorised competent person scheme under [COMPETENT PERSON SCHEME REGISTRATION NUMBER].

Two things worth insisting on from any electrician. Notifiable work in a home must be registered — through a competent person scheme or with building control — and you should receive a compliance certificate, because without it a buyer's solicitor or an insurer has nothing to rely on. And an EICR records condition on the day: it is not a warranty against future faults, and a report with a C1 or C2 cannot honestly be marked satisfactory. We do not carry out gas work, which needs a Gas Safe registered engineer.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

How often does a rented property need an EICR?

At least every five years in England, with the report held and given to tenants and, on request, the local authority. That has been the law since 2020 and it is enforced by councils.

My EICR came back unsatisfactory. Is that normal?

It is common in older housing, and it means something coded C1 or C2 was found — danger or potential danger. We will not mark a report satisfactory where one applies, and the remedial work is quoted separately so you can compare prices.

Do I need the council involved for a new socket?

Some work is notifiable and some is not. Where it is, our scheme registration lets us self-certify and notify, and you get a compliance certificate. Ask any electrician whether they can do that.

Can you just replace the tripping breaker?

Not without finding why it trips. A breaker doing its job is a symptom; swapping it removes the protection and leaves the fault. We test to locate it first.

Can I have an EV charger on my existing supply?

Often yes, and sometimes not. A charge point draws a sustained load, so the main fuse, board and earthing all need assessing first. We will tell you honestly if an upgrade is needed before you order a car charger.

Do you do gas as well?

No. Gas work requires a Gas Safe registered engineer and we are electricians. We will coordinate rather than take it on.
